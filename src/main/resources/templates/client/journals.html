ON MEMORY & LANGUAGE

Why We Return to Books We've Already Read

There is a particular kind of re-reading that has nothing to do with forgetting. It happens when a book finds you at a different angle of your life — when the words are unchanged but you are not. This is what separates literature from information: only the former can read you back.

A

The Architecture of a First Sentence

Every first sentence contains within it the entire book — not as summary, but as tone. The reader agrees to something in those opening words, an unspoken contract about the kind of attention they will need to give. The great ones make refusal impossible.

The Ethical Collector: When Ownership Meets Obsession

To collect books is to confess a belief that the physical object carries something beyond its text. Yet there is a threshold where curation curdles into hoarding, where the shelves become a performance of a self rather than an expression of one. Where is that line?
